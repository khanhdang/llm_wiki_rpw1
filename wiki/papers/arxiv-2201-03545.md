ConvNeXt starts from a standard ResNet-50 and "modernizes" it step by step
toward the design of a hierarchical vision Transformer (Swin), without adding
any attention. The result is a pure ConvNet family that matches or beats Swin
Transformers on ImageNet, COCO and ADE20K while keeping ConvNet simplicity
[src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.1] [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.2]. The authors conclude
that much of the reported gap between ConvNets and Transformers came from
training recipes and design details, not from self-attention itself
[src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3] [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.9].

Liu, Mao, Wu, Feichtenhofer, Darrell, Xie. *A ConvNet for the 2020s.*
arXiv:2201.03545, 2022.

## Contribution

- **A controlled comparison.** ConvNets and hierarchical Transformers share
  similar inductive biases but differ in training procedure and in macro and
  micro design. The paper isolates those confounding variables instead of
  comparing whole systems [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.2].
- **The ConvNeXt family.** T, S, B, L and XL variants, built entirely from
  standard ConvNet modules [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.2]
  [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.6].
- **Nothing new on its own.** The authors stress that every design choice has
  been studied before, separately but never together
  [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.6].

## Method: the modernization roadmap

ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy at ResNet-50 / Swin-T scale (about 4.5 GFLOPs),
averaged over three seeds [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.2]
[src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3]:

| Step | Change | Top-1 | Source |
|------|--------|-------|--------|
| 0 | ResNet-50, original training | 76.1% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3] |
| 1 | Transformer-style training: 300 epochs, AdamW, Mixup, Cutmix, RandAugment, Random Erasing, stochastic depth, label smoothing | 78.8% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3] |
| 2 | Stage compute ratio (3, 4, 6, 3) → (3, 3, 9, 3), as in Swin | 79.4% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3] |
| 3 | "Patchify" stem: 4×4 conv, stride 4 | 79.5% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3] |
| 4 | ResNeXt-ify: depthwise conv, width 64 → 96 | 80.5% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.4] |
| 5 | Inverted bottleneck (4× expansion) | 80.6% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.4] |
| 6 | Move the depthwise conv up (intermediate step) | 79.9% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.4] |
| 7 | 7×7 depthwise kernel; gains saturate at 7×7 | 80.6% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.4] |
| 8 | ReLU → GELU | 80.6% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5] |
| 9 | One activation per block | 81.3% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5] |
| 10 | One normalization layer per block | 81.4% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5] |
| 11 | BatchNorm → LayerNorm | 81.5% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5] |
| 12 | Separate 2×2 stride-2 downsampling layers, with LN where resolution changes | 82.0% | [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5] |

Swin-T's reference accuracy is 81.3% [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5].

- **The training recipe matters.** The recipe alone gave +2.7 points, which
  the authors take to mean that a large share of the ConvNet–Transformer gap
  may come from training techniques [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3].
- **Separate spatial and channel mixing.** Depthwise convolution mixes only
  across space, like the weighted sum in self-attention. Paired with 1×1
  convolutions, it separates spatial from channel mixing, as Transformers do
  [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.4].
- **Unstable downsampling.** Separate downsampling layers made training
  diverge until normalization layers were added wherever the resolution
  changes [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.5].
- **Variants.** Channels C = (96, 192, 384, 768) with blocks B = (3, 3, 9, 3)
  for T, and (3, 3, 27, 3) blocks for S, B, L and XL, with wider channels for
  the larger models [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.6].
- **Training.** AdamW with learning rate 4e-3, batch size 4096, weight decay
  0.05, Layer Scale 1e-6 and EMA [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.6]. EMA badly hurts
  models that still use BatchNorm [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.9].

## Results

- **ImageNet-1K training.** ConvNeXt-T 82.1% vs Swin-T 81.3% at 4.5 GFLOPs.
  ConvNeXt-B 83.8% vs Swin-B 83.5% at 224² [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.7]. At
  384², ConvNeXt-B scores 85.1% vs 84.5%, with 12.5% higher throughput
  [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.7].
- **ImageNet-22K pre-training.** ConvNeXt-L at 384² scores 87.5% vs 87.3% for
  Swin-L, and ConvNeXt-XL reaches 87.8% [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.7]. The
  authors read this as evidence that ConvNets are not inferior at large
  pre-training scale [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.7].
- **Isotropic (ViT-style) ConvNeXt.** On par with ViT: 82.0% vs 81.8% at
  B size, and 82.6% for both at L size [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.7].
- **COCO, Cascade Mask R-CNN.** ConvNeXt-B‡ scores 54.0 box AP vs 53.0 for
  Swin-B‡, and ConvNeXt-XL‡ scores 55.2 [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.8].
- **ADE20K, UperNet.** ConvNeXt-B‡ scores 53.1 mIoU vs 51.7 for Swin-B‡
  [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.8].
- **Memory.** Cascade Mask R-CNN training uses 17.4 GB peak with ConvNeXt-B
  vs 18.5 GB with Swin-B [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.8].
- **Robustness.** ConvNeXt-XL (22K) scores 69.3%, 68.2% and 55.0% on
  ImageNet-A, -R and -Sketch, with no specialized modules
  [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.10].

> [!conflict]
> ConvNeXt's tables give slightly different Swin numbers from the Swin paper.
> COCO Cascade Mask R-CNN, Swin-T: 50.4 box AP [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.8] vs
> 50.5 [src:arxiv_2103.14030 p.8]. ADE20K, Swin-T: 45.8 mIoU vs 46.1; Swin-B‡:
> 51.7 vs 51.6 [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.8] [src:arxiv_2103.14030 p.7].
> ConvNeXt says its ImageNet-1K Swin detection and segmentation numbers come
> from Swin's GitHub repository [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.8].

## Limitations

- **Not universal.** The authors suggest Transformers may suit some tasks
  better, such as multi-modal learning with cross-attention or tasks with
  discretized, sparse or structured outputs [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.12].
- **Better ConvNets on ImageNet-1K alone.** EfficientNetV2-L, a searched
  architecture with progressive training, still scores higher on ImageNet-1K
  (85.7%) [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.7].
- **Stage ratios not optimized.** The stage compute ratio was copied from
  Swin, and a better distribution likely exists [src:arxiv_2201.03545 p.3].
