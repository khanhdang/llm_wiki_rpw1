This 2020 survey covers CNN building blocks, classic models from LeNet-5 to
GhostNet, and experiments comparing activation functions and optimizers. It
also covers 1D, 2D and multi-dimensional applications, and prospects such as
compression, security, architecture search and capsule networks
[src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.1] [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.2]. As a secondary source,
it is useful for orientation, but its descriptions of individual papers
should be checked against the primary sources (see the conflicts below).

Li, Yang, Peng, Liu. *A Survey of Convolutional Neural Networks: Analysis,
Applications, and Prospects.* arXiv:2004.02806, 2020.

## Contribution

- **Scope.** The survey treats CNNs from a general perspective, where earlier
  reviews focused on particular applications. It also covers newer models such
  as MobileNet v3, Inception v4 and ShuffleNets [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.1]
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.2].
- **Experiments.** It runs its own comparisons of activation functions and
  optimizers and draws rules of thumb from them [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.2].

## Method (what it covers)

- **History.** The MP neuron (1943), the perceptron, backpropagation networks
  (1986), TDNN (1987, a 1D CNN), and LeCun et al.'s 1989 network for zip codes,
  which first used the term "convolution" [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.1].
- **Building blocks.** Convolution, padding, stride and pooling, plus dilated
  and deformable convolution [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.2].
- **LeNet-5 (1998).** Seven trainable layers: two convolutional, two pooling
  and three fully connected. It combined local receptive fields, shared
  weights and subsampling, but did not beat SVMs or boosting at the time
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.3]. The original paper disputes the SVM claim; see
  the conflict on [[doi-10-1109-5-726791]].
- **AlexNet (2012).** Eight layers: five convolutional and three fully
  connected. The survey credits it with ReLU, dropout, local response
  normalization, overlapping max pooling, two-GPU group convolution, and data
  augmentation by random crops and PCA colour shifts
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.3].
- **Later models.** VGGNets, Inception v1–v4, ResNet
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.4], DCGAN, and MobileNet v1/v2
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.5].

## Results (its own experiments)

- **Activation functions.** Tested with LeNet-5 and VGG-16 on MNIST,
  FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.8]. Findings:
  sigmoid and tanh are hard to converge when fine-tuning a pre-trained deep
  model. Leaky ReLU and ELU were most accurate, but ELU sometimes made networks
  learn nothing, so Leaky ReLU is the more reliable choice
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.10]. (Table I is misaligned in the extraction, so no
  per-setting numbers are quoted here.)
- **Optimizers.** Ten optimizers were compared with pre-trained VGG-16 on
  CIFAR-10. Almost all converge, mini-batch gradient descent converges
  slowest, and the learning rate strongly affects convergence
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.13]. (Table III merges the optimizers and their
  accuracies into a single cell in the extraction.)
- **Rules of thumb.** Use ReLU or Leaky ReLU in hidden layers
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.11]. If training oscillates or diverges, lower the
  learning rate [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.14].
- **Outlook.** The survey discusses model compression (low-rank approximation,
  pruning, quantization) [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.16]; data poisoning and
  adversarial attacks; architecture search; and capsule networks
  [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.17] [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.18].

## Limitations

- **Secondary source.** Some of its summaries of other papers disagree with
  those papers:

> [!conflict]
> The survey says ResNet "can mitigate the gradient vanishing problem"
> [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.5]. The ResNet paper argues that the degradation it
> addresses is "unlikely to be caused by vanishing gradients", because
> batch normalization keeps forward signals and gradients healthy
> [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.5].

> [!conflict]
> The survey says MobileNet v1 uses depthwise separable convolutions
> "proposed in Xception" [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.5]. The MobileNets paper says
> they were "initially introduced in [26]", and that Xception later showed how
> to scale them up [src:arxiv_1704.04861 p.1].

- **Its own experiments.** Small-scale (LeNet-5 and VGG-16 on
  MNIST/CIFAR-sized data) and run once [src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.8].
