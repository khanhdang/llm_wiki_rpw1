This paper introduces residual learning: blocks of layers learn a residual
F(x) = H(x) − x, and an identity shortcut adds the input x back. This makes
very deep networks trainable. Residual networks of up to 152 layers, 8× deeper
than VGG but cheaper to run, won ILSVRC 2015 classification with 3.57% top-5
error [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.1].

He, Zhang, Ren, Sun. *Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition.*
arXiv:1512.03385, 2015.

## Contribution

- **The degradation problem.** As plain networks get deeper, accuracy first
  saturates and then degrades. This is not overfitting: training error itself
  goes up [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.1].
- **Residual learning with identity shortcuts.** The shortcuts add no
  parameters or computation [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.2]. See
  [[residual-learning]].
- **Results.** First place in ILSVRC 2015 classification, ImageNet detection,
  ImageNet localization, COCO detection and COCO segmentation
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.1].

## Method

- **Why it should work.** A deeper model can match a shallower one by making
  its extra layers identity mappings, so its training error should be no
  higher. Plain networks still fail to find such solutions in practice
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.1]. If identity is close to optimal, pushing a
  residual to zero should be easier than fitting identity with non-linear
  layers [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.2] [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.3].
- **Building block.** y = F(x, {Wᵢ}) + x, with the ReLU applied after the
  addition. When dimensions change, a linear projection W_s on the shortcut
  matches them [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.3]. (Equations 1–2 were not decoded in
  the extraction; this is the form given in the prose.)
- **Plain baseline.** Follows VGG: mostly 3×3 filters, the same number of
  filters for the same map size, and filters doubled when the map size halves.
  Downsampling uses stride-2 convolutions, and the network ends with global
  average pooling [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.3]. The 34-layer network costs
  3.6 billion FLOPs, 18% of VGG-19's 19.6 billion [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.3].
- **Shortcut options** when dimensions increase: (A) zero-padded identity,
  with no parameters; (B) projection; (C) projections everywhere
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.4] [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6].
- **Bottleneck block.** ResNet-50/101/152 use 1×1, 3×3, 1×1 blocks in which
  the 1×1 layers reduce and then restore dimensions
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6]. ResNet-152 costs 11.3 billion FLOPs, less than
  VGG-16/19 at 15.3/19.6 billion [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.7].
- **Training.** Batch normalization after every convolution, SGD with batch
  size 256 and learning rate 0.1 (divided by 10 on plateaus), weight decay
  0.0001, momentum 0.9, and no dropout [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.4].

## Results

- **Plain vs residual, top-1 validation error, 10-crop.** Plain: 27.94% at 18
  layers, 28.54% at 34. ResNet: 27.88% at 18, 25.03% at 34. Depth helps only
  with residual connections [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.5].
- **Not vanishing gradients.** The authors argue the plain-net problem is not
  vanishing gradients, since batch normalization keeps forward signals and
  gradient norms healthy. They conjecture that deep plain networks converge
  exponentially slowly [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.5].
- **Shortcut options.** Top-1 error was 25.03% for A, 24.52% for B and 24.19%
  for C, so projections are not essential [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6].
- **Single models, validation.** Top-5 error of 6.71% for ResNet-50, 6.05% for
  ResNet-101 and 5.71% for ResNet-152 with 10-crop testing
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6]. ResNet-152 reaches 4.49% top-5 at multiple
  scales, better than any earlier ensemble [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6]
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.7].
- **Ensemble.** 3.57% top-5 test error from six models, against 6.66% for
  GoogLeNet and 7.32% for VGG at ILSVRC'14 [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6]
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.7].
- **CIFAR-10.** A 110-layer ResNet reached 6.43% error with 1.7M parameters
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.7]. A 1202-layer ResNet trained to under 0.1%
  training error but scored 7.93% on test [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.8].
- **Residual responses.** Layer responses in ResNets are generally smaller
  than in plain networks, which supports the idea that residuals stay near
  zero [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.8].
- **Detection.** Swapping VGG-16 for ResNet-101 in Faster R-CNN raised COCO
  mAP@[.5,.95] from 21.2 to 27.2, a 28% relative gain
  [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.8].

## Limitations

- **Cause unexplained.** Why plain networks are hard to optimize is left for
  future work [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.5].
- **Overfitting at extreme depth.** The 1202-layer network does worse on test
  than the 110-layer one, which the authors attribute to overfitting on a small
  dataset without strong regularization [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.8].
- **Bottleneck design.** Chosen for practical reasons, to keep training time
  affordable [src:arxiv_1512.03385 p.6].
