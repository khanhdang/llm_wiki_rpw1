This 1998 paper argues that recognition systems should rely more on
automatic learning and less on hand-designed heuristics
[src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.1]. It makes the case in two places. For single
characters, convolutional networks, especially LeNet-5, learn their features
from pixels and beat all other methods compared on a standard handwritten digit
benchmark [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.1]. For whole documents, Graph
Transformer Networks (GTNs) train multi-module systems end to end with
gradient-based learning. A GTN-based check reader was deployed commercially
and reads several million checks per day [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.1].

LeCun, Bottou, Bengio, Haffner. *Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document
Recognition.* Proceedings of the IEEE, 1998 (DOI 10.1109/5.726791). The
venue and year are taken from [src:arxiv_1409.4842 p.11] and
[src:arxiv_2004.02806 p.3]; the OCR text of the paper has no date line.

## Contribution

- **Learned features beat hand-crafted ones.** Hand-crafted feature
  extraction can be replaced by learning machines that operate directly on
  pixel images [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.1].
- **Convolutional networks.** They combine local receptive fields, shared
  weights and spatial sub-sampling to get some shift, scale and distortion
  invariance [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.6]. Because all weights are learned,
  the network synthesizes its own feature extractor
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.7]. See [[convolutional-neural-network]].
- **MNIST.** A new benchmark built by mixing NIST's SD-1 (high-school
  students) and SD-3 (Census Bureau employees), with 60,000 training patterns
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.10].
- **Graph Transformer Networks.** A framework for training multi-module
  document recognition systems globally with gradient-based methods
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.1].

## Method: LeNet-5

- **Layers.** 7 layers, not counting the input, all with trainable
  parameters. The input is a 32×32 image, larger than the largest character
  (at most 20×20 in a 28×28 field) [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.7].
- **C1.** 6 feature maps of 28×28, each unit connected to a 5×5 neighbourhood;
  156 parameters [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.7].
- **S2.** Sub-sampling to 6 maps of 14×14. Each unit sums 2×2 inputs, scales
  them by a trainable coefficient, adds a trainable bias and applies a
  sigmoid [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.7].
- **C3.** 16 maps, each connected to only a subset of S2's maps. This keeps
  connections in bounds and breaks symmetry; 1,516 parameters
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.8]. (Table I, the connection scheme, is
  garbled in the extraction.)
- **S4.** Sub-sampling to 16 maps of 5×5 [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.8].
- **C5.** 120 maps of 1×1 over all of S4, which amounts to a full
  connection. It is labelled convolutional because a larger input would make
  its maps larger than 1×1 [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.8].
- **F6.** 84 units fully connected to C5; 10,164 parameters
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.8].
- **Activation.** A scaled hyperbolic tangent with amplitude A = 1.7159
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.8].
- **Output.** One Euclidean radial basis function (RBF) unit per class, each
  measuring the distance from F6 to a fixed parameter vector of ±1 entries
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.8].
- **Size.** 340,908 connections but only 60,000 trainable parameters, thanks
  to weight sharing [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.7].
- **Training.** Back-propagation with weight sharing: gradients of all
  connections that share a parameter are summed. Optimization is a stochastic
  diagonal Levenberg-Marquardt method [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.9]. A
  discriminative loss stops the RBF centres collapsing when they are learned
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.9].
- **Schedule.** 20 passes over MNIST, with the global learning rate stepped
  down from 0.0005 to 0.00001 [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.10].

## Results (MNIST test set, 10,000 examples)

- **LeNet-5.** Test error settles at 0.95% after about 10 passes; training
  error reaches 0.35% after 19 [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.10].
- **Distortions.** Adding 540,000 artificially distorted training images
  (affine translations, scaling, squeezing, shearing) lowers test error to
  0.8%. The 82 remaining errors are mostly ambiguous digits or styles that
  are rare in training [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.11].
- **More data helps.** Training on 15k, 30k and 60k examples shows that more
  data would still improve accuracy [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.10].
- **Comparison with other classifiers** (test error):

| Method | Error | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Linear classifier | 12% (8.4% deslanted) | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.11] |
| K-nearest neighbour, Euclidean | 5.0% (2.4% deslanted) | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.12] |
| 1-hidden-layer net, 300 / 1000 units | 4.7% / 4.5% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.13] |
| RBF network | 3.6% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.13] |
| PCA + quadratic classifier | 3.3% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.12] |
| LeNet-1 (about 2,600 parameters) | 1.7% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.13] |
| SVM, regular | 1.4% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14] |
| Tangent distance classifier (16×16) | 1.1% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14] |
| Reduced-set SVM | 1.1% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14] |
| Virtual SVM (modified) | 0.8% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14] |
| LeNet-5 with distortions | 0.8% | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14] |
| Boosted LeNet-4 | 0.7% (best) | [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14] |

The uncertainty in these error rates is about 0.1%
[src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.12].

- **Cost.** A regular SVM needs about 14 million multiply-adds per
  recognition. The reduced-set SVM needs 650,000, about 60% more than LeNet-5
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.14]. Neural networks need far less memory and
  computation than memory-based methods [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.15].
- **Training time.** 2–3 days of CPU for LeNet-5 on a single 200 MHz R10000
  processor [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.15].
- **Check reading.** On 646 machine-printed business checks, the GTN system
  read 82% correctly with 1% errors and 17% rejected, against 68% correct,
  1% errors and 31% rejected for the previous system
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.39].

## Limitations

- **The authors' own caveats.** LeNet-5 suited 1998's computers and data, as
  LeNet-1 suited 1989's. A network as large as LeNet-5 would not have been
  practical in 1989 [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.15]. The training-size
  experiment shows the model is still data-limited
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.10].
- **SVMs come close.** SVMs reach comparable accuracy without any prior
  knowledge of images, though only at a much higher memory and compute cost
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.15].
- **Narrow evaluation.** The paper evaluates on isolated digits (MNIST) and
  document tasks. Large-scale natural-image recognition with CNNs came later;
  see [[arxiv-1409-0575]].
- **Partial coverage of this page.** The GTN, segmentation, SDNN and online
  handwriting sections (pp.16–37) are only summarized here, at the level of
  the abstract and conclusions [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.1]
  [src:doi_10.1109_5.726791 p.40].
