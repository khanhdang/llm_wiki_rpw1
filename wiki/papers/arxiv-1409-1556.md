The VGG paper tests how depth affects a convolutional network's accuracy,
holding the design fixed and stacking only 3×3 convolutions, up to 16–19
weight layers. Its networks took first place in ILSVRC-2014 localization and
second in classification, and transferred well to other datasets
[src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.1].

Simonyan, Zisserman. *Very Deep Convolutional Networks for Large-Scale Image
Recognition.* arXiv:1409.1556, 2014.

## Contribution

- A controlled study of depth: configurations A–E share one design and differ
  only in depth, from 11 weight layers (8 conv + 3 FC) to 19 (16 conv + 3 FC)
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.2].
- The argument for small filters: two stacked 3×3 layers see a 5×5 region and
  three see 7×7. Three 3×3 layers with C channels use 27C² weights against
  49C² for one 7×7 layer, and add two more non-linearities
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.2].

## Method

- **Architecture.** Input is a 224×224 RGB image with the mean subtracted. All
  convolutions are 3×3 with stride 1 and padding that keeps the resolution;
  there are five 2×2 max-pooling layers. Three fully connected layers (4096,
  4096, 1000) end in a softmax, and every hidden layer uses ReLU
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.2].
- **Width.** Starts at 64 channels and doubles after each pooling layer, up to
  512 [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.2]. The networks have 133–144 M parameters
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.3].
- **Training.** Mini-batch SGD with momentum 0.9, batch size 256, weight decay
  5·10⁻⁴, and dropout 0.5 on the first two FC layers. The learning rate starts
  at 10⁻² and drops 10× three times; training stops after 370K iterations
  (74 epochs) [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.4].
- **Initialization.** Deeper networks take their first four conv layers and
  their FC layers from the shallow net A. The authors later found that Glorot
  and Bengio's random initialization works without this pre-training
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.4].
- **Scale jittering.** Each training image is rescaled so that its shorter side
  S is random in [256, 512] [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.4].
- **Dense evaluation.** At test time the FC layers become convolutions, the
  network runs over the whole image, and the class score map is averaged
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.5].
- **Hardware.** Data-parallel training on four NVIDIA Titan Black GPUs took
  2–3 weeks per network [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.5].

## Results

- **Depth.** Error falls as depth grows from A to E and levels off at 19
  layers. Local response normalization does not help. A shallow net with 5×5
  filters had top-1 error 7% higher than net B [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.6].
- **Single scale.** Top-1/top-5 validation error was 29.6/10.4% for A and
  25.5/8.0% for E with scale jittering [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.6].
- **Best single network.** 24.8%/7.5% top-1/top-5 on validation with
  multi-scale testing, and 7.3% top-5 on test for configuration E
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.7].
- **Ensemble.** The ILSVRC submission of 7 networks scored 7.3% top-5 test
  error. After submission, 2 networks reached 6.8%, against 6.7% for the
  winner, GoogLeNet ([[arxiv-1409-4842]]). A single VGG network scored 7.0%,
  0.9 points better than a single GoogLeNet [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.8].
- **Localization.** Won ILSVRC-2014 with 25.3% test error
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.11].
- **Transfer.** Features from these networks set the state of the art on
  VOC-2007, VOC-2012, Caltech-101 and Caltech-256 [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.12]
  [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.13].

## Limitations

- **Cost.** With 133–144 M parameters [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.3] and 2–3 weeks
  of training per network on four GPUs [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.5], the networks
  are expensive.
- **Depth limit.** Accuracy saturated at 19 layers on this dataset, although
  deeper models might help on larger datasets [src:arxiv_1409.1556 p.6].
  [[arxiv-1512-03385]] later identified a degradation problem when plain
  networks get deeper.
