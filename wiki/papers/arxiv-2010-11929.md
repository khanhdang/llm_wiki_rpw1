This paper shows that a plain Transformer, applied to a sequence of image
patches with no convolutions, classifies images very well when pre-trained on
enough data. The resulting Vision Transformer (ViT) matches or beats strong
CNNs on ImageNet, CIFAR-100 and VTAB while using much less pre-training
compute [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.1]. On mid-sized datasets alone it trails
ResNets, because it lacks the CNN's built-in inductive biases
[src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.1].

Dosovitskiy, Beyer, Kolesnikov, Weissenborn, Zhai, Unterthiner, Dehghani,
Minderer, Heigold, Gelly, Uszkoreit, Houlsby. *An Image is Worth 16x16 Words:
Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale.* arXiv:2010.11929, 2020.

## Contribution

- **A standard Transformer for images.** Image patches are treated like
  word tokens, with as few changes to the architecture as possible
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.1]. See [[vision-transformer]].
- **Scale beats inductive bias.** With 14M–300M pre-training images, ViT
  approaches or beats the state of the art [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.2].

## Method

- **Patches as tokens.** The image is cut into N = HW/P² patches of P×P
  pixels. Each is flattened and linearly projected to dimension D, giving the
  patch embeddings [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.3].
- **Class token and positions.** A learnable [class] token is prepended, and
  its final state is the image representation. Learnable 1D position
  embeddings are added; 2D-aware variants gave no significant gain
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.3].
- **Encoder.** Standard Transformer layers alternate multi-head self-attention
  and MLP blocks (two layers with GELU). LayerNorm comes before each block and
  a residual connection after it [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.3]
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.4].
- **Inductive bias.** Only the MLP layers are local and translation
  equivariant; self-attention is global. The 2D structure enters only at
  patch extraction and when position embeddings are interpolated for a new
  resolution [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.4].
- **Hybrid.** Alternatively, patches can be taken from a CNN feature map
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.4].
- **Fine-tuning.** Swap in a zero-initialized head, often at higher
  resolution than pre-training, with the patch size unchanged and the
  position embeddings interpolated in 2D [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.4].
- **Model sizes.** ViT-Base: 12 layers, D = 768, 86M parameters. ViT-Large:
  24 layers, 1024, 307M. ViT-Huge: 32 layers, 1280, 632M. "ViT-L/16" means
  Large with 16×16 patches [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.5].
- **Pre-training data.** ImageNet (1.3M images), ImageNet-21k (14M) and JFT
  (303M) [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.4].
- **Training.** Adam with batch size 4096 and weight decay 0.1. Adam beat SGD
  even for the ResNet baselines in this setting [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.5].

## Results

- **Best model.** ViT-H/14 pre-trained on JFT-300M reaches 88.55% on ImageNet,
  90.72% on ImageNet-ReaL, 94.55% on CIFAR-100 and 77.63% on VTAB (19 tasks)
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.2].
- **Pre-training cost (TPUv3-core-days).** 2.5k for ViT-H/14 and 0.68k for
  ViT-L/16 on JFT, and 0.23k for ViT-L/16 on ImageNet-21k. The CNN baselines
  took 9.9k (BiT-L) and 12.3k (Noisy Student) [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.6].
  ViT-L/16 on JFT outperforms BiT-L on every task [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.5].
- **Public data only.** ViT-L/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21k reaches 85.30% on
  ImageNet [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.6]. It can be trained on a standard
  8-core cloud TPUv3 in about 30 days [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.5]. (Table 2 is
  partly misaligned in the extraction, but this column reads cleanly.)

> [!conflict]
> [src:arxiv_2103.14030 p.6] lists ImageNet-22K-pretrained ViT-L/16 at 85.2%
> top-1 at 384². This page reports 85.30% from the ViT paper
> [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.6]. The gap is small and may be rounding or a
> different evaluation run.

- **Data requirements.** Pre-trained on ImageNet only, ViT-Large does worse
  than ViT-Base and BiT ResNets beat ViT. With ImageNet-21k the two sizes are
  similar, and only with JFT-300M do larger models pay off
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.6].
- **Subsets of JFT.** On 9M images ViT overfits more than comparable ResNets,
  but it is better from 90M upward [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.7].
- **Compute.** ViT needs about 2–4× less pre-training compute than ResNets for
  the same transfer performance. Hybrids help only at small sizes, and ViT
  shows no saturation in the range tested [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.8].
- **What it learns.** Position embeddings come to encode 2D distance. Some
  heads attend globally even in the lowest layers, and attention distance
  grows with depth [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.8].
- **Self-supervision.** Masked-patch prediction gives ViT-B/16 79.9% on
  ImageNet: 2 points better than training from scratch, 4 points behind
  supervised pre-training [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.8].

## Limitations

- **Needs large-scale pre-training.** On mid-sized data without strong
  regularization it is a few points below comparable ResNets
  [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.1].
- **Classification only.** Detection and segmentation are left as open
  challenges [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.9]. [[arxiv-2103-14030]] (Swin) targets
  these dense tasks.
- **Self-supervision gap.** A large gap remains between self-supervised and
  supervised pre-training [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.9].
- **Patch size and cost.** Sequence length grows with the inverse square of
  the patch size, so small patches are expensive [src:arxiv_2010.11929 p.5].
