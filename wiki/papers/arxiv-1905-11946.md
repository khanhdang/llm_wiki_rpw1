The authors find that balancing a network's depth, width and input resolution
gives better accuracy and efficiency than scaling any one of them. They
propose compound scaling, which scales all three together with a single
coefficient. Applied to a baseline found by architecture search
(EfficientNet-B0), it yields EfficientNet-B1 to B7. B7 reaches 84.3% top-1 on
ImageNet while being 8.4× smaller and 6.1× faster than the previous best model
[src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.1].

Tan, Le. *EfficientNet: Rethinking Model Scaling for Convolutional Neural
Networks.* ICML 2019 (PMLR 97) [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.1]; arXiv:1905.11946.

## Contribution

- **Quantifying the link.** The authors claim to be the first to measure how
  width, depth and resolution relate when scaling a network
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.2].
- **[[compound-scaling]].** A principled alternative to scaling one dimension
  by hand [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.1].
- **The EfficientNet family.** Models that use an order of magnitude fewer
  parameters and FLOPS than earlier ConvNets of similar accuracy
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.7].

## Method

- **Observation 1.** Scaling up any single dimension improves accuracy, but
  the gain shrinks as models grow. In single-dimension scaling, accuracy
  saturates around 80% [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.4].
- **Observation 2.** The dimensions depend on each other: higher-resolution
  input needs more depth for a larger receptive field and more width for
  finer patterns, so all three must be balanced [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.4].
- **Compound scaling.** Depth scales by α^φ, width by β^φ and resolution by
  γ^φ, under the constraint α·β²·γ² ≈ 2, so total FLOPS grow by about 2^φ.
  FLOPS are proportional to d·w²·r² [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.1]
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.5]. (Equation 3 was not decoded in the extraction;
  this is the form given in the prose.)
- **Baseline search.** EfficientNet-B0 comes from a multi-objective
  architecture search optimizing ACC(m)×[FLOPS(m)/T]^w with w = −0.07 and a
  400M FLOPS target. It is similar to MnasNet and built mainly from mobile
  inverted bottleneck (MBConv) blocks [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.5].
- **Two steps.** First fix φ = 1 and grid-search α = 1.2, β = 1.1,
  γ = 1.15. Then fix those values and increase φ to get B1–B7. Searching only
  once, on the small model, keeps the cost down [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.5].
- **Training.** RMSProp; SiLU (Swish-1) activation; AutoAugment; stochastic
  depth with survival probability 0.8; and dropout rising from 0.2 (B0) to
  0.5 (B7) [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.6] [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.7].

## Results

- **ImageNet top-1.** B0 scores 77.1% with 5.3M parameters and 0.39B FLOPS,
  against 76.0% for ResNet-50 with 26M and 4.1B. B7 scores 84.3% with 66M
  parameters, matching GPipe, which has 557M [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.6].
- **B1 and B3.** B1 scores 79.1% against 77.8% for ResNet-152, with 7.6× fewer
  parameters [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.6]. B3 beats ResNeXt-101 with 18× fewer
  FLOPS [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.7].
- **Compound scaling on existing networks.** MobileNetV1 goes from 70.6% to
  75.6%, against 74.2% when scaled by width only. ResNet-50 goes from 76.0% to
  78.8%, against 78.1% when scaled by depth only [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.6].
- **Compound scaling on B0.** At about 1.8B FLOPS it reaches 81.1%, against
  roughly 79% for depth, width or resolution alone [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.8].
- **CPU latency (single core, batch size 1).** B1 runs 5.7× faster than
  ResNet-152 (0.098 s vs 0.554 s), and B7 6.1× faster than GPipe (3.1 s vs
  19.0 s) [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.6].
- **Transfer.** State of the art on 5 of 8 datasets with 9.6× fewer
  parameters on average, for example 91.7% on CIFAR-100 and 98.8% on Flowers
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.7].
- **Test vs validation.** Test-set accuracy closely matches validation, for
  example 84.33% vs 84.26% top-1 for B7 [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.9].

## Limitations

- **Dependence on the baseline.** How well scaling works depends heavily on
  the baseline network, which is why a new one was searched for
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.2].
- **Mixed causes.** Better architectures, better scaling and training
  settings tuned for EfficientNet all contribute to the gains
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.7]. Figure 8 tries to isolate the scaling effect
  [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.8].
- **Coefficients found on the small model.** α, β and γ are searched on the
  small model only. Searching around a large model might do better but costs
  too much [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.5].
- **FLOPS, not latency.** The search optimizes FLOPS rather than latency on any
  particular hardware [src:arxiv_1905.11946 p.5].
